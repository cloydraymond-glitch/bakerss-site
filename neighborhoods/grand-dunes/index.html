Bermuda turf management

Lawn Care That Understands Grand Dunes Turf Conditions

The majority of established estate lawns in Grand Dunes are Bermuda grass — a warm-season turf that performs well in full-sun coastal conditions but demands precise management at seasonal transitions. Bermuda goes dormant in the winter months, greens up aggressively in spring and requires properly timed pre-emergent herbicide applications to prevent crabgrass and summer annual weed pressure from getting ahead of the recovery.

Along the Myrtle Beach coast, pre-emergent timing targets soil temperatures consistently above 50–55°F — which typically puts the first application window at February 1–15 for Grand Dunes properties. Miss that window and weed pressure from crabgrass and goosegrass becomes difficult to recover from without damaging the Bermuda stand.

Bakerss Myrtle Beach lawn care schedules for Grand Dunes are built around actual turf conditions, not a generic calendar. Mowing height is kept at 1–1.5 inches during peak growing season. Edge lines are crisp to HOA standards. Blowing keeps hardscapes, driveways and entries clean after every service. For properties with irrigation systems, we coordinate service timing to avoid double-watering on mow days.

If you are an absentee owner and your Grand Dunes property sits unattended between seasonal visits, a recurring maintenance plan ensures the lawn, beds, pool and exterior surfaces never fall behind HOA compliance thresholds while you are away.
