About Garden City Beach

Garden City Beach — Between Two Identities, Distinctly Its Own

Garden City Beach is an unincorporated community in Horry County situated between Surfside Beach to the north and the Murrells Inlet marsh to the south — a geographic position that gives it a dual-influence character unlike any other Grand Strand community. Its name derives from the garden-like character of its original beachfront development, a deliberate contrast to the commercial resort development that defined central Myrtle Beach's growth. The community's unincorporated status — meaning it falls under Horry County governance rather than a municipal government — has been one of the primary reasons the community has preserved its residential character and modest scale through decades of Grand Strand development pressure.

The Garden City Pier is the community's defining landmark. Having survived — or been rebuilt following — multiple significant hurricane events, the Pier's persistence through these tests is itself a statement about the community's character and attachment to its identity. The Garden City Connector bridge forms the community's northern boundary with Surfside Beach, and the Murrells Inlet marshes define the southern edge. Between those two boundaries, approximately 9,000 residents and a growing vacation rental market occupy a stretch of Atlantic-facing coastline that offers direct beach access without the resort-corridor intensity of communities to the north.

Deck Construction at Garden City Beach

What Maximum Atlantic Exposure Means for GCB Deck Specification

Garden City Beach's direct Atlantic positioning — at maximum salt air concentration with the additional marsh humidity from the south — creates the most demanding fastener corrosion environment in the southern Grand Strand. Standard zinc-plated hardware in this location corrodes visibly within 18–24 months. By year three, rust is migrating into surrounding wood at every fastener location, creating the rot pathways that make board replacement necessary well before the decking material has worn out. The Garden City Pier's repeated destruction by hurricane events is a useful reminder that nothing in this community is built with indifference to the coastal environment — and deck construction should reflect the same awareness.

Stainless steel fasteners throughout — every screw, every joist hanger, every post base connector — is the baseline specification for any Garden City Beach deck built to last. The cost difference between stainless and zinc-plated hardware on a standard deck is $250–$500. The cost of rebuilding a deck eight years early because zinc fasteners failed the structure is $8,000–$25,000. This is not a close decision.

Material selection at Garden City Beach also benefits from honest acknowledgment of the storm exposure history. Composite decking handles storm events better than wood — it doesn't absorb the water that organic materials do during prolonged storm flooding, and it doesn't warp or swell from storm moisture in the way that pressure-treated wood can. For oceanfront and near-ocean Garden City Beach properties, composite is not just the preferred maintenance choice — it is the storm-resilient choice.
