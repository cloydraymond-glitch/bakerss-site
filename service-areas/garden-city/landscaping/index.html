About Garden City Beach

Garden City Beach — Between Two Identities, Distinctly Its Own

Garden City Beach is an unincorporated community in Horry County situated between Surfside Beach to the north and the Murrells Inlet marsh to the south — a geographic position that gives it a dual-influence character unlike any other Grand Strand community. Its name derives from the garden-like character of its original beachfront development, a deliberate contrast to the commercial resort development that defined central Myrtle Beach's growth. The community's unincorporated status — meaning it falls under Horry County governance rather than a municipal government — has been one of the primary reasons the community has preserved its residential character and modest scale through decades of Grand Strand development pressure.

The Garden City Pier is the community's defining landmark. Having survived — or been rebuilt following — multiple significant hurricane events, the Pier's persistence through these tests is itself a statement about the community's character and attachment to its identity. The Garden City Connector bridge forms the community's northern boundary with Surfside Beach, and the Murrells Inlet marshes define the southern edge. Between those two boundaries, approximately 9,000 residents and a growing vacation rental market occupy a stretch of Atlantic-facing coastline that offers direct beach access without the resort-corridor intensity of communities to the north.

Landscaping for Maximum Coastal Exposure

What Survives — and Thrives — in Garden City Beach's Salt Air

Garden City Beach's name — derived from the garden-like character of its original beachfront development — sets a standard of intentional beauty that the community's residential properties can honor or undermine through their landscape choices. A properly designed and installed landscape using the right plants for maximum coastal exposure creates the curb appeal that makes a GCB property stand out among its neighbors. A landscape installed with the wrong plant palette — species that cannot handle direct Atlantic salt air — requires replacement within two to three years and looks progressively worse as those plants decline.

Garden City Beach's direct Atlantic position at the southern end of the Grand Strand's oceanfront corridor subjects every planted landscape to salt air conditions that eliminate many popular ornamental species. Japanese maples — beautiful in sheltered gardens — scorch and defoliate within one summer near the ocean. Standard boxwood, lavender, and many popular groundcovers simply don't survive the combination of salt air, summer heat, and humidity that characterizes this specific coastal environment.

The plant palette that thrives at Garden City Beach is well-established through decades of successful coastal planting in this community. Sea oats and native grasses for coastal edge areas. Indian Hawthorn for foundation planting — salt-tolerant, bloom reliably, handles the conditions without struggle. Knock Out Roses that bloom from March to November in coastal conditions. Dwarf Yaupon Holly for structure that handles salt air without complaint. Crepe myrtles in the size appropriate to each space. Native ornamental grasses — Muhly Grass, Switchgrass — that provide fall color and seasonal interest without demanding anything from the challenging environment. Liriope for groundcover that handles both salt air and shade from the structure above.

Indian Hawthorn, Knock Out Roses, Dwarf Yaupon Holly, Crepe Myrtle, Liriope, Muhly Grass, Asian Jasmine, Sea Oats (coastal areas). Species to avoid: Japanese Maple, standard Boxwood, Lavender. Every species we install has demonstrated performance in maximum Grand Strand coastal conditions.

Does Garden City Beach being unincorporated affect landscaping?+
Yes — positively. No municipal HOA approved species list constrains your choices. The design constraint is the coastal environment (what survives here) rather than bureaucratic standards. We take full advantage of that design freedom within the performance constraints the salt air creates.
Do you provide ongoing landscape maintenance in Garden City Beach?+
