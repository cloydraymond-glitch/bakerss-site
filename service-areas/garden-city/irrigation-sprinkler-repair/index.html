Common sprinkler symptoms

When Garden City Properties Need Irrigation Repair

Sprinkler problems usually show up in the lawn before the system owner knows which part failed. The goal is to connect the symptom to the right repair path instead of guessing, overwatering, or letting sod decline through another hot week.

Brown Areas Beside Wet Areas

One broken head, clogged nozzle or misaligned spray pattern can soak one section while another section stays dry. This is common around driveways, sidewalks, pool areas and narrow rental-property lawns.

Zones That Do Not Come On

A dead zone may involve controller settings, wiring, a valve issue, a stuck component or a water supply problem. A clear quote defines diagnostic limits before repair work begins.

Sprinklers Hitting Concrete

Overspray wastes water and can stain driveways, sidewalks, fences, siding and storefront glass. Adjustment or head replacement may redirect water back to turf and beds.

Low Pressure or Weak Spray

Low pressure can come from leaks, clogged heads, valve issues, pipe restrictions or system design limitations. The repair path depends on what is visible and testable onsite.

Timer Schedules That Do Not Match the Lawn

New sod, established centipede, landscape beds, sandy soil and shaded areas do not all need the same schedule. Timer review can prevent overwatering and underwatering.

Leaks Near Heads, Valves or Buried Lines

Wet spots, bubbling, erosion, washed-out mulch or a meter that keeps moving can point to a leak. Access, depth and part availability affect the final repair scope.
