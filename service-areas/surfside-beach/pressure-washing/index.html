About Surfside Beach — The Family Beach

Surfside Beach, SC — Understanding the Community We Serve

Surfside Beach incorporated in 1951 — one of the last Grand Strand communities to formalize — and has governed itself since that founding with a deliberate, specific goal: preserving the residential, family-oriented character that distinguishes it from the resort-scale development corridor to the north. With approximately 4,500 permanent residents in roughly one square mile, Surfside Beach is the smallest municipality on the Grand Strand. The Town Council's consistent land use decisions have kept the commercial scale modest and the residential identity intact for more than seven decades.

This governance philosophy creates a distinctive property market. Many Surfside Beach properties have been in the same family for two and three generations — a grandparent bought the 1960s beach cottage, the children grew up vacationing in it, and now the grandchildren manage it as a vacation rental. This multigenerational ownership pattern means many Surfside properties carry 50 and 60 years of use, renovation, and personal history. It also means the guest base for Surfside vacation rentals is among the most loyal on the Grand Strand — the same family returning to the same property every summer for a decade or more. That loyalty is worth protecting through consistent professional maintenance.

The Surfside Washing Priority

How Pressure Washing Protects Surfside Beach's Older Properties

Pressure washing in Surfside Beach serves two distinct purposes that are more consequential for this community than for many other markets. First, it restores appearance — the visible mildew, salt film, and tannin staining that accumulates on every Surfside exterior needs removal to keep properties looking maintained. Second, and more importantly for Surfside's older housing stock, it removes the accumulation that accelerates material degradation. Salt retained against older wood siding creates the persistent moisture condition that leads to rot. Mildew colonizing paint creates the biological acid that penetrates paint film and shortens the effective lifespan of every paint job it reaches.

For a family property that has been maintained for thirty or forty years, professional exterior washing every six to twelve months is the protective maintenance that has preserved the structure through decades of use. It is not an optional cosmetic service — it is the baseline exterior care that every Surfside Beach property requires.

Soft Wash vs Pressure Wash — The Right Method for Surfside

The critical distinction for Surfside Beach's older housing stock is that all siding must be soft washed — never high-pressure washed. High pressure applied to older vinyl siding, wood siding, or painted wood forces water behind the cladding and into wall assemblies that were not built with modern moisture barriers. Older Surfside properties are particularly vulnerable: the building envelope practices of 1965 or 1975 construction were designed for the conditions of that era, not for direct water pressure assault. We soft wash all siding surfaces at under 500 PSI using chemistry that kills mildew and removes salt film without the pressure that damages older materials. High pressure is reserved exclusively for concrete driveways, walkways, and masonry — surfaces that can handle it.

Siding — All Types

Soft Wash Standard

All siding types soft washed at under 500 PSI with surfactant and mildewcide chemistry. Kills mildew at the root rather than just displacing it — results last longer. Wood, vinyl, fiber cement, and stucco all receive soft wash. No exceptions for older Surfside construction.

Driveways & Walkways

High-Pressure Concrete Cleaning

Concrete driveways, walkways, and patios receive 2,500–3,500 PSI high-pressure washing to remove salt deposits, tannin staining from neighboring vegetation, and the biological growth that darkens concrete over time. The surface difference before and after is immediate and dramatic.

Decks

Material-Appropriate Washing

Wood decks cleaned at low-to-medium pressure before staining. Composite decks under 1,500 PSI to preserve surface. Concrete and paver pool decks and patios at appropriate high pressure. Material-specific approach — never one pressure for everything.
