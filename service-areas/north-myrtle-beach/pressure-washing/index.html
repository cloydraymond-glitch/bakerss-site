Built for salt film, resort traffic, and HOA visibility

North Myrtle Beach has several exterior-cleaning environments inside one city. Cherry Grove and Ocean Drive properties sit close enough to the Atlantic for salt film and wind-driven sand to bond to windows, siding, railings, pool decks, and concrete. Barefoot Resort properties deal with golf-course pollen, irrigation overspray, shaded driveways, and HOA curb appeal expectations. Tidewater Plantation combines marsh moisture, mature tree canopy, and gated-community presentation standards. A generic pressure-washing page misses those differences. This page is built for how North Myrtle Beach properties actually get dirty.

We use pressure where pressure belongs — concrete, pavers, certain hardscape surfaces — and soft washing where pressure can cause damage: vinyl siding, painted trim, stucco, composite surfaces, soffits, and coastal railings. Surface Safety

Soft washing vs pressure washing — we use the correct method

Soft Wash

For siding, stucco, soffits, trim, and paint

Soft washing uses low pressure and cleaning solution to kill organic growth at the root. It is the right method for most North Myrtle Beach house exteriors, especially vinyl siding, painted surfaces, shutters, and trim.

Pressure Wash

For concrete and hard surfaces

Higher pressure belongs on concrete, certain pavers, and hardscape surfaces where algae, tire marks, and grime require mechanical cleaning. We do not blast fragile surfaces to make a job faster.
