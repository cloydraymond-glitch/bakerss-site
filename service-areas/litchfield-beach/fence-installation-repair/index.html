Fence repairs near the coast: inspect the failure before choosing materials

Coastal exposure, saturated soils, wind and age can create very different fence problems. A leaning fence section may have compromised posts or footings, while a dragging gate can result from loose hardware or a shifted opening. Repairs should be based on what has actually failed—not simply replacing the visible panel.

Posts and ground contact

Inspect post movement, rot at grade, footing stability and drainage before promising a straightened fence will stay straight.

Gates and hardware

Check hinges, latches, clearances and attachment points. Hardware appropriate for humid, salt-influenced environments may reduce repeat problems.

Community and boundary approval

Confirm who owns the fence, whether an HOA review applies and whether setbacks, permitted locations or utility marking are required before installation.

Fence materials can have different maintenance requirements. The quote should identify repair versus replacement, disposal, finish work and any exclusions. For nearby gates, decks or trim, handyman services may be scoped separately; regulated or structural work must follow applicable requirements.
