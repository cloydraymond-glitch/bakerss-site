Coastal landscape planning

What Makes a Strong Litchfield Beach Landscape?

A strong Litchfield Beach landscape begins with the property’s real conditions: sun exposure, shade, sandy soil, irrigation coverage, drainage patterns, salt-air exposure, plant maturity, bed size, palm growth, guest traffic and service expectations. A landscape can look good on the day it is installed and still fail if the plantings are wrong, irrigation does not reach the bed, mulch washes out, edges are not maintained or weeds take over.

Bakerss approaches landscaping as part of property maintenance, not just a one-time visual upgrade. The goal is to improve the exterior while making it easier to maintain. That may mean refreshing mulch before peak season, converting high-maintenance beds to rock, trimming palms and shrubs before they crowd walkways, adding plantings where the property needs texture, coordinating sod where turf has failed, or connecting the project with irrigation repair so new material does not dry out.

For second homes and vacation rentals, this matters because the owner may not see the property every week. For HOAs and commercial properties, it matters because multiple people judge the same shared exterior. For private residences, it matters because clean landscaping improves curb appeal and makes the whole property feel better maintained.

Materials & options

Mulch, Pine Straw, Rock Beds, Plants, Palms & Sod Support

Different landscape materials perform differently near the coast. The right choice depends on budget, appearance, maintenance expectations, tree cover, drainage and how often the property is serviced.

Mulch Installation

Mulch gives beds a clean, finished look and helps define edges. It is a strong option for homes and rentals that receive recurring maintenance and seasonal refreshes.

Pine Straw

Pine straw can fit natural Lowcountry landscapes and larger beds. It may be useful where a softer, natural appearance is preferred.

Decorative Rock

Rock can reduce frequent refresh needs in select beds, but it should be planned carefully around heat, drainage, plant health and maintenance access.

Coastal Plantings

Plant selection should account for sun, salt air, sandy soil, irrigation and maintenance. The wrong plant in the wrong place creates callbacks and replacements.

Palms & Shrubs

Palms, shrubs and ornamental grasses need trimming that improves shape without damaging long-term appearance or blocking walkways and windows.

Sod & Lawn Edges

Landscape beds connect to turf. Thin lawn edges, irrigation gaps and poor transitions can make even new beds look unfinished.
