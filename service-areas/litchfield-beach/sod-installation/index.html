Why sod prep matters

Sod Fails When Soil, Water, Drainage & Turf Selection Are Ignored

New sod can look excellent the day it is installed and still decline if the conditions underneath it are wrong. Poor watering coverage, compacted soil, bad drainage, heavy shade, weak prep or the wrong turf type can create thin spots, browning, disease pressure and customer frustration.

Turf selection

Choosing the Right Sod for Litchfield Beach Property Conditions

The right turf depends on the yard. Bakerss looks at sun, shade, irrigation, use, soil, expectations and follow-up maintenance before making a recommendation.

Centipede Sod

Often considered for lower-maintenance warm-season lawns where the property conditions and care expectations fit.

Bermuda Sod

Can perform in high-sun, higher-use areas when mowing, watering and maintenance expectations are compatible.

Zoysia Sod

A strong curb appeal option for certain lawns, but site conditions, cost, watering and maintenance should be reviewed first.

Before installing sod

The Most Expensive Sod Mistake Is Installing Before the Yard Is Ready

New sod is perishable and establishment-sensitive. If the irrigation does not cover the area, if the soil is not prepared, if drainage is not considered, or if the yard is not ready when sod arrives, the lawn can decline quickly. Bakerss scopes the work around the real condition of the property so the installation has a practical chance to establish.
