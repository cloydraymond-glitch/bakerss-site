About Little River

Serving the Little River Community

Little River's history stretches back to the 18th century when it served as a major commercial fishing port on the Grand Strand. The community retains much of that authentic fishing village character today — a welcome contrast to the more developed resort strip to the south. Little River is home to a fleet of deep-sea fishing charter boats, a busy marina district, and the famous Blue Crab Festival held each May. The town sits at the confluence of the Intracoastal Waterway and the Little River Inlet, making waterfront property a defining feature of the community. In recent decades, Little River has attracted significant residential development as retirees and remote workers seek the coastal lifestyle without Myrtle Beach's tourist intensity.

Little River's waterfront position — both on the Intracoastal and near the ocean inlet — creates demanding maintenance conditions. Salt air exposure is significant, marine-grade humidity is constant, and the community's mix of older fishing-village structures and newer waterfront homes spans a wide range of maintenance needs. Properties along the water face accelerated paint degradation, corrosion, mildew growth, and the ongoing maintenance demands of docks and waterfront structures. Inland residential properties still benefit from the Grand Strand's subtropical climate advantages and challenges — fast-growing lawns, heavy seasonal rainfall, and a long active maintenance season.

Little River has a distinctive property mix: waterfront homes on the ICW and inlet with boathouses and docks, established residential neighborhoods in the interior, newer retirement communities and golf course developments, and vacation rental properties that benefit from the area's authentic character and proximity to the ocean. Bakerss serves all of these property types with services tailored to each community's needs.
