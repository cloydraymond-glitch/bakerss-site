📍 Little River, SC: Blue Crab Capital of the Grand Strand

Little River is an unincorporated waterfront community at the northernmost point of Horry County, adjacent to the North Carolina border. It sits on the Little River Inlet and the Intracoastal Waterway, giving it some of the most beautiful waterfront access on the entire Grand Strand.

The community is known for its Blue Crab Festival, which has drawn visitors to Little River every May since 1975 — making it one of South Carolina's longest-running food festivals. Evidence of Native American settlement at Waites Island near Little River has been documented, including burial mounds and pottery shards, placing human habitation here for thousands of years.

Today, Little River features a marina district, waterfront dining, sportfishing charter operations, and a mix of waterfront estates and residential neighborhoods. The proximity to water is everything here — and it’s why moisture preparation is critical for every concrete surface in the community.

🦀 Epoxy’s Original Application: Marine Protection

It’s fitting that Little River — a maritime community built around the water — benefits from technology with marine roots. Epoxy was first developed in the early 20th century specifically for marine use: protecting ship hulls and decks from the exact same saltwater and humidity exposure that Little River’s concrete faces every day. The chemistry has been refined over 90 years, but the fundamental application — protecting surfaces from moisture, salt, and chemical attack — is unchanged. Your garage sits in the same environment those early marine chemists were trying to solve.

Does Little River’s waterfront location affect concrete floors?+
Significantly. Little River’s position on the Intracoastal Waterway and Little River Inlet means properties here experience some of the highest salt air and moisture exposure in Horry County. Concrete in waterfront and near-waterfront properties requires additional moisture vapor transmission testing and a robust moisture barrier primer. Our prep process addresses this systematically — no assumptions, no shortcuts.

How long does epoxy last?+
With 100% solids commercial epoxy installed correctly on a diamond-ground, moisture-primed surface, 20–30 years is a realistic lifespan for residential garage use. Big-box water-based products typically peel within 1–3 years. The difference is entirely in the solids content, surface preparation, and system design.
