Conway-Specific Landscaping

Landscaping Built for Inland Horry County

Conway properties deal with a different landscape profile than Myrtle Beach, Surfside, or Murrells Inlet. The lots are often larger, the tree coverage is heavier, the soil commonly holds more clay, and many neighborhoods have deeper front yards, longer driveways, and larger side setbacks. That creates more opportunity for landscape impact — but it also creates more risk if drainage, soil prep, edging, and plant selection are ignored.

Bakerss approaches Conway landscaping around the way Conway properties actually behave: runoff from heavy rain, shaded turf under oak and pine canopy, new-construction yards with thin builder-grade topsoil, and HOA communities where clean bed lines and curb appeal matter every week.

Conway Soil & Drainage

Why Drainage and Soil Prep Matter in Conway

Conway’s inland soil profile often includes sandy-clay mixes that compact, hold water after heavy rainfall, and create uneven growth. Landscaping that ignores drainage may look good for a week and fail over the season. Before installing beds, shrubs, sod, or curbing, we look at where water moves, where beds collect runoff, where downspouts discharge, and where shade prevents turf from recovering.

Problem

Standing Water Near Beds

Water collecting at foundation beds can drown plants, create mulch washout, and push moisture toward crawl spaces or slabs.

Solution

Drainage-Aware Layout

We shape beds, select materials, and recommend grading or downspout adjustments before installing plants or curbing.

Problem

Builder-Grade Soil

New Conway homes often have compacted construction soil that limits root development and causes patchy turf.

Solution

Prep Before Planting

We improve soil where needed, use appropriate plant spacing, and avoid overbuilding beds that will be hard to maintain.
