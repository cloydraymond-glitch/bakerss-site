About Murrells Inlet — SC's Seafood Capital

Murrells Inlet — The Community We Serve

Murrells Inlet is the Seafood Capital of South Carolina — an unincorporated waterfront community in Horry County approximately 10 miles south of Myrtle Beach along the tidal inlet system that gives it its name. The community's history stretches back centuries: the Waccamaw people inhabited this stretch of South Carolina's Lowcountry well before European contact, and the land that is now Murrells Inlet was part of the antebellum rice plantation system that made coastal South Carolina one of the most productive agricultural regions in 18th and 19th century North America. Brookgreen Gardens — opened in 1931 as the oldest public sculpture garden in the United States — occupies 9,100 acres across four former rice plantations just south of the inlet, a National Historic Register property that draws over 350,000 visitors annually.

The Murrells Inlet Marshwalk — a half-mile boardwalk lined with seafood restaurants including Drunken Jack's, Creek Ratz, and Dead Dog Saloon — is one of the most photographed waterfront destinations in South Carolina and the social center of a community that has evolved from a working fishing village into one of Horry County's most desirable residential addresses. The Prince Creek corridor along Highway 707 — encompassing TideWater Plantation, Wachesaw Plantation East, and Collins Creek Landing — represents Murrells Inlet's premium residential market, with property values from $300,000 to over $1,000,000 for custom waterfront homes.

Murrells Inlet's Unique Washing Demands

Three Forces That Make MI Exteriors Require More Aggressive Washing

1. Live Oak Tannin Staining — Murrells Inlet's Defining Surface Challenge

The 100+ year old live oaks throughout Wachesaw Plantation East and the Prince Creek corridor deposit tannin compounds in a quantity and persistence that no other Horry County community experiences. Tannins are water-soluble compounds in oak bark, leaves, and acorns that wash out in rain — and when that water hits a concrete driveway or walkway below, the tannins bond with the concrete surface and oxidize into the brown-to-black staining that is characteristic of properties under heavy live oak coverage. On a Wachesaw Plantation driveway directly under the canopy, this staining can develop to near-black discoloration within a single season without intervention. High-pressure washing with appropriate concrete cleaning chemistry effectively removes this staining. Nothing else does.

2. Marsh Environment Mildew — Continuous Biological Growth

Murrells Inlet's tidal marsh environment creates humidity conditions that sustain mildew and algae growth on exterior siding and paint surfaces year-round. This is not a summer phenomenon that resolves in winter — the marsh microclimate maintains the moisture conditions that mildew requires even when temperatures fall. Properties adjacent to or near tidal areas experience the most aggressive mildew conditions. Soft washing with mildewcide chemistry kills the organisms at the root — producing results that last longer than pressure rinsing because the biology that creates the staining is eliminated rather than temporarily displaced.

3. Salt Air from the Atlantic — The Background Coastal Condition

Murrells Inlet's position 10 miles from the Atlantic still places it within meaningful salt air influence. Salt particles from the ocean deposit on exterior surfaces throughout the community — creating the hygroscopic moisture layer on paint that accelerates degradation and the general surface film that washing removes. The combination of salt air, marsh humidity, and tannin deposits creates a triple-accumulation condition that makes annual professional washing essential for maintaining MI property appearance at the level the community's premium positioning demands.

Decks

Canopy Debris Treatment

Wood and composite decks under live oak canopy accumulate a combination of tannin staining, organic debris, and the moisture-retained biological growth that heavy shade creates. Deck washing with material-appropriate method — low-to-medium pressure on wood before restaining, under 1,500 PSI on composite — restores appearance and prepares surfaces for treatment.

How often should Murrells Inlet properties be pressure washed?+
Wachesaw Plantation and properties under heavy live oak canopy: twice yearly (spring and fall). Standard Prince Creek and outer MI properties: annually. Post-tropical storm: immediately after any significant weather event regardless of regular schedule.
What causes the dark brown/black staining on Wachesaw driveways?+
Live oak tannin compounds deposited by the 100+ year old oaks and washed onto concrete surfaces by rain. Tannins bond with concrete and oxidize to dark brown-to-black color within a single season under heavy canopy coverage.
