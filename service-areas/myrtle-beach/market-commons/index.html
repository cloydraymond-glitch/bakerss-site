Neighborhood Profile

Market Common — From Air Force Base to Urban Village

Myrtle Beach Air Force Base operated from 1940 until 1993, occupying prime acreage in the geographic center of Myrtle Beach. Its closure created one of the largest land redevelopment opportunities in South Carolina history. Burroughs & Chapin — the region's largest private landowner and a company with deep roots in the Grand Strand dating to the early 20th century — acquired the site and spent years planning what would become Market Common.

Construction began in the mid-2000s with the first residential products delivering in 2008. The community features townhomes built primarily by local and regional builders in the $200,000–$500,000 range, along with single-family cottages closer to the central retail district. Architecture reflects a new-urbanist design philosophy — porches facing walkable streets, rear-loading garages, mixed housing types on the same block, and a density that creates genuine pedestrian activity.

The Runway Park — a linear park following the former taxiway — is the community's primary green space and gathering place. Warbird Park preserves several historic aircraft. The town square anchors the retail district with national brands (Target, Bed Bath & Beyond) alongside local dining and boutiques. Market Common has become one of Myrtle Beach's most sought-after addresses for primary residents who value the walkable character over beachfront proximity.

Maintenance Profile

Market Common Maintenance — High Visibility, Active HOA

Market Common's walkable density means every property is visible from the street and from neighboring homes at close range — a fundamentally different maintenance context than low-density suburban neighborhoods. The active HOA enforces appearance standards appropriate for this visible, pedestrian-active environment. Lawn care, bed maintenance, and exterior appearance are all more closely observed here than in conventional neighborhoods. Weekly professional lawn care during growing season and annual exterior washing are the standard for keeping Market Common properties aligned with community character.
