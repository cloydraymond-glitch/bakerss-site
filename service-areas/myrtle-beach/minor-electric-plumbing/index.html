How Minor Repairs Protect Myrtle Beach Rental Revenue & Home Comfort

In Myrtle Beach's vacation rental market, minor electrical and plumbing issues have a revenue dimension that primary residence owners don't face to the same degree. A running toilet at a vacation rental isn't just a water bill issue — it's a sound that keeps guests awake, a condition they photograph and mention in reviews, and the kind of overlooked maintenance detail that signals to discerning guests that the property is not as well-managed as the listing implied. Myrtle Beach's competitive STR market means guests have options, and the well-maintained property consistently wins repeat bookings over the comparable property that tolerates small deferred maintenance.

For primary residents, the same minor electrical and plumbing issues have comfort and cost dimensions. A leaking faucet that drips at 1 drip per second wastes over 3,000 gallons annually — showing up in Myrtle Beach's water bills as a consistent $30–$60 annual drain on a problem that costs $125–$225 to fix. A GFCI outlet that won't reset after a storm trip can disable a bathroom circuit — a nuisance for primary residents and a genuine problem for vacation rental guests.

The GFCI Reality in Myrtle Beach's Coastal Humidity

Ground fault circuit interrupter outlets are required in all bathrooms, kitchens, garages, and outdoor circuits in Myrtle Beach homes — and they trip more frequently in the coastal humidity environment than inland properties. The combination of high indoor humidity and the electrical demand of vacation rental guest usage creates nuisance tripping patterns that confuse guests and require either remote reset guidance (which guests often can't successfully follow) or a physical visit. GFCI outlets that trip repeatedly should be replaced — a nuisance-tripping GFCI indicates degraded internal components. We carry common GFCI sizes on every vehicle for immediate replacement.
