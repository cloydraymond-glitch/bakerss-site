The Coastal Deck Challenge

Why Deck Construction Is Different in Myrtle Beach

Every deck built in Myrtle Beach operates in a coastal environment that most standard building specifications were not designed for. Salt particles from the Atlantic deposit continuously on every surface. Combined with the persistent high humidity of South Carolina's subtropical coast, this creates an electrochemical environment that corrodes standard zinc-plated fasteners — the hardware that holds deck boards and structural framing together — visibly within three to four years. The corrosion migrates into surrounding wood, creating rot pathways at every fastener location and resulting in deck boards that need individual replacement well before the decking material itself has worn out.

We use stainless steel fasteners throughout every deck we build in Myrtle Beach. This is not an upgrade or a premium option — it is the baseline specification for any deck expected to perform for its full potential lifespan in this environment. The cost difference between stainless and zinc-plated hardware on a standard deck is $200–$400. The cost of rebuilding a deck eight years early because zinc fasteners failed the wood is $8,000–$20,000. The specification choice is straightforward.

Composite Decking

Our First Recommendation for Myrtle Beach

Capped composite decking (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) with stainless steel fasteners is our primary recommendation for Myrtle Beach. No staining required. No sealing required. Handles beach sand, salt air, and pool splashing without degradation. Resists mold and mildew. 25–30 year lifespan with annual cleaning. Higher upfront cost — $35–$65/sq ft installed — but lowest lifetime cost when the maintenance savings are calculated.

Pressure-Treated Wood

With Coastal Specifications

Pressure-treated pine with stainless steel fasteners and correct coating selection remains a viable and more affordable option ($20–$35/sq ft installed). The critical difference from standard PT wood construction: stainless fasteners throughout, appropriate stain/sealer within 30 days of installation, and realistic maintenance commitment (restaining every 2–3 years in Myrtle Beach's climate). PT wood decks that are properly maintained last 15–20 years here.

FAQ

Deck Building FAQ — Myrtle Beach

What decking material is best for Myrtle Beach's salt air environment?+
Capped composite decking (Trex, TimberTech) with stainless steel fasteners is our primary recommendation. It is immune to salt air, handles moisture and sand without degradation, never needs staining or sealing, and delivers 25–30 years of performance. Pressure-treated wood with stainless fasteners is the more affordable alternative — it performs well if properly maintained (staining every 2–3 years).
How much does a deck cost in Myrtle Beach?+
New composite deck construction starts around $7,000 for a small deck (200 sq ft) and runs $10,500–$32,500 for standard sizes. Pressure-treated wood decks start around $4,000. Permits, hardware, and inspections included in all quotes. Free estimates for your specific scope.
Do you pull Horry County permits for deck construction?+
Yes — all deck construction in Myrtle Beach requires Horry County permits, and we handle the entire permit process. Application, any required engineering documentation, submission, and coordination of all required inspections are managed by us. You do not need to interact with the county permitting office.
Why are stainless fasteners so important in Myrtle Beach?+
Standard zinc-plated screws and hardware corrode visibly in Myrtle Beach's salt air within 3–4 years. The rust migrates into surrounding wood, creating rot pathways at every fastener location. Board replacement then becomes necessary well before the decking material itself has worn out. Stainless steel performs for 20+ years in this environment — the $200–$400 hardware cost difference prevents thousands in premature repair costs.
Do you repair existing decks in Myrtle Beach?+
Yes. We replace individual boards, repair or replace railings, upgrade fasteners from zinc to stainless, and address structural issues including post and beam repair. If your deck can be economically repaired rather than replaced, we'll tell you that honestly rather than recommending unnecessary rebuilding.
