Why Myrtle Beach Is Different

What Coastal Humidity Does to a Bad Garage Floor — and Why It Happens Here More Than Anywhere

📍 Myrtle Beach Climate Reality

Myrtle Beach records an average annual relative humidity of 85% at 4am — one of the highest overnight humidity levels on the East Coast. During peak months (May, June, August, December), that number stays elevated all day. Concrete is porous. Moisture vapor transmits through it continuously. Water-based epoxy products — the type sold at every hardware store on US-17 — cannot withstand sustained moisture vapor transmission. They lift, bubble, and peel. We've removed failed big-box epoxy from garages across the Grand Strand every season. The fix costs more than doing it correctly the first time. 100% solids commercial epoxy applied over a diamond-ground surface with a moisture barrier primer is the only system that holds in this climate.

What We Install
🏆

100% Solids Commercial Epoxy — The Only Correct Spec for Coastal SC

Myrtle Beach's subtropical humidity is the reason 100% solids is non-negotiable here. Water-based products (30–50% solids) leave gaps in the film that moisture vapor exploits. 100% solids fills those gaps. Combined with a moisture barrier primer and diamond-ground concrete profile, this is the system that delivers 20+ years in the Myrtle Beach climate. It's the same specification used in NASCAR garage facilities, aircraft maintenance hangars, and commercial food processing plants — environments with continuous chemical and moisture exposure.

💧

Moisture Testing — Required in This Market

We test every slab before we coat. Myrtle Beach's proximity to the water table makes moisture vapor transmission unpredictable. Concrete slabs poured in the 1990s and 2000s — the era of most Grand Strand residential construction — are particularly susceptible.

Diamond Grinding — Non-Negotiable

We grind every slab with industrial diamond tooling. Acid etching — the method used by most DIY installers — does not create adequate mechanical profile on Myrtle Beach concrete. Salt air contributes to surface mineral deposits that block chemical bonding. Grinding removes all of it.

The Science Behind the Product

Epoxy Has Been Proven for
90 Years. Here's Why.

The chemistry in your Myrtle Beach garage floor has roots in some of the most demanding applications in human history. Understanding why epoxy became the global standard explains why cutting corners on it is always the wrong call.

Early 1900s

Russian Chemists & Marine Applications

Russian scientists first experimented with epoxy compounds primarily as a primer to help paint adhere to ship hulls in harsh marine environments — the original coastal application. The problem: saltwater and humidity cause conventional coatings to fail rapidly. Epoxy was developed specifically to solve this.

1936

Pierre Castan Synthesizes the First Commercial Epoxy Resin

Swiss chemist Dr. Pierre Castan, alongside American Dr. S.O. Greenlee, developed and patented the first commercial epoxy resin. Their discovery — combining bisphenol A and epichlorohydrin with a hardener — created a material recognized immediately for its exceptional adhesion, chemical resistance, and durability.

WWII

Military Installations — Protecting Concrete from Heavy Machinery

The U.S. military adopted epoxy flooring in bases and installations worldwide to protect concrete surfaces from heavy vehicle traffic, chemical exposure, and continuous mechanical load. The performance demands of military use proved epoxy's long-term durability in ways no civilian application could.

1950s–60s

Commercial & Industrial Dominance

Automotive manufacturers, aerospace facilities, and food processing plants standardized on epoxy flooring. The industries that demanded the most from their floors — and could most afford to test alternatives — all chose epoxy. That consensus has never changed.

NASA Era

Space Capsule Floors

NASA used epoxy coatings on space capsule floors for their non-slip surface properties and chemical resistance. When the standards are "must not fail while astronauts are weightless at 17,500 mph," the material has been validated at a level no residential floor demands.

Today

Your Myrtle Beach Garage

The same chemistry — refined over 90 years of industrial use — is the system we install in Myrtle Beach garages. The product has not changed in its fundamental performance properties because nothing has been found to outperform it at the specification level we install.
