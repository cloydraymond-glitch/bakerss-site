Choosing the right sod for Myrtle Beach

Centipede, Bermuda, Zoysia or St. Augustine: Which Sod Makes Sense?

The best Myrtle Beach sod installation is not just the prettiest pallet available that week. Grass selection should match sun exposure, irrigation coverage, traffic, soil condition, shade, maintenance expectations and how the property is used. Bakerss helps homeowners, HOA boards, apartment managers and commercial owners think through the practical difference before the lawn is replaced.

Centipede Sod

Often used for Myrtle Beach residential lawns because it can fit lower-maintenance expectations when sunlight, soil prep and watering are managed correctly. Good for many standard home lawns, but it still needs proper establishment care.

Bermuda Sod

Best considered for full-sun, higher-traffic areas where aggressive growth is useful. Bermuda can recover well from wear, but it generally expects consistent mowing, irrigation and maintenance discipline.

Zoysia Sod

A premium curb-appeal option for properties where density, appearance and texture matter. Zoysia can be a strong choice for higher-standard homes, HOAs and commercial frontage with proper care.

St. Augustine Sod

A broad-blade warm-season option that may suit certain coastal and partially shaded conditions. It is site-dependent, so Bakerss reviews water, shade, drainage and maintenance expectations before recommending it.

New sod fails when the cause of the old lawn failure is ignored.

If the original grass died from poor irrigation, drainage, shade, soil compaction, pet traffic, fungus, grub damage, salt stress or neglect, replacing it with sod alone may not solve the problem. Bakerss looks for the reason the turf failed before pricing the installation, then recommends prep, grading, watering and follow-up care that give the new lawn a better chance to establish.

Myrtle Beach sod establishment

What Happens After Sod Is Installed Matters as Much as Installation Day

The first few weeks after installation determine whether a new Myrtle Beach lawn roots properly. Heat, sandy soil, missed watering, foot traffic, mower timing and irrigation gaps can damage sod before it has a chance to establish.

Install Day

Soil contact and watering

Sod should be installed tightly with clean seams, good soil contact and immediate watering. Dry edges and gaps are corrected before cleanup.

Week 1

Moisture management

The goal is consistent moisture without creating standing water. Irrigation coverage should be checked because missed zones show quickly in Myrtle Beach heat.

Weeks 2–3

Rooting and traffic control

Traffic should be limited while roots begin to tack down. Pool areas, rental paths, pets and HOA common areas may need extra protection.

After Establishment

Mowing and maintenance

Once the sod has rooted, mowing height, watering frequency and ongoing lawn care need to match the grass type and property conditions.
