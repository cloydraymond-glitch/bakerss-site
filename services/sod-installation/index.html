Why Full-Service Installation

A Finished Lawn — Not Pallets of Sod in Your Driveway

Sod is only as good as the ground beneath it. The sandy, acidic soils across Myrtle Beach, Conway, and the Grand Strand drain fast and run low on nutrients — lay sod over unprepared ground and it shows within a season. That's why every Bakerss sod installation is a complete project: we strip out the old grass and weeds, grade the site for drainage, apply starter fertilizer matched to the variety, lay every roll tight, press it for soil contact, water it in, and haul away every scrap of debris.

From Carolina Forest to Murrells Inlet, we install the four warm-season grasses proven in Horry County's nine-to-ten month growing season — and we'll tell you honestly which one fits your yard's sun, shade, soil, and traffic before you spend a dollar.

Questions

Sod Installation FAQ

How much does sod installation cost in Myrtle Beach?

Complete installations start at $3,500 for a small lawn (~1,500 sq ft) in Centipede, including grass removal, grading, starter fertilizer, installation, rolling, watering, and cleanup. Average lawns start at $6,500 and large lawns at $10,500, with Bermuda, St. Augustine, and Zoysia priced higher. Most quality projects land between $4,000–$12,000+ all-in.

What's the best sod for coastal South Carolina?

Centipede is the coastal workhorse — happy in our acidic, sandy soil with minimal feeding. Bermuda wins in full sun and traffic, St. Augustine handles shade with a premium look, and Zoysia is the dense luxury option. We recommend based on your actual yard, not what's on the truck.

When should sod be installed in Horry County?

Warm-season sod roots best from late spring through early fall, but our nine-to-ten month growing season makes installation viable most of the year. Spring and early summer installs establish fastest.

Do you remove the old grass first?

Always. Stripping the old turf and weeds, then grading, is what separates a lawn that thrives from sod that fails in a season — it's included in every quote.

How long before I can use the lawn?

Stay off it for 2–3 weeks while roots take hold. With daily watering in the first two weeks, sod here typically roots in 10–14 days and is fully established in 4–6 weeks.
