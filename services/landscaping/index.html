Why Local Expertise Matters

Landscaping in Myrtle Beach Is Different Than Inland South Carolina

The Grand Strand creates a demanding landscape environment. Salt air dries and burns sensitive foliage. Sandy soil drains quickly and often lacks organic matter. Humidity increases disease pressure. Heavy summer rain can wash mulch out of beds, expose roots and create drainage problems. Tropical systems can flatten poorly selected ornamentals. HOA communities expect clean, visible curb appeal. Vacation rental properties need landscapes that look sharp for guests but do not require constant owner supervision.

That is why generic plant lists fail here. A plant that performs well inland may struggle near Ocean Boulevard, Garden City Beach, Surfside Beach, North Myrtle Beach or the Intracoastal Waterway. Bakerss selects materials based on site conditions: full sun versus shade, salt exposure, irrigation availability, soil condition, bed slope, drainage, mature plant size and maintenance expectations.

Concrete Landscaping Curbing

Permanent Concrete Curbing for Clean Bed Borders

Concrete landscape curbing can create a durable, defined border between lawn and planting beds. Where this specialty service is available, the scope, finishes, site preparation and drainage implications should be confirmed before installation.

In coastal South Carolina, concrete curbing has a major advantage over plastic or thin metal edging. Plastic can buckle, shift and pull loose. Metal can corrode in salt-air conditions. Concrete remains stable, visible and low maintenance when installed properly. It is especially valuable for front beds, driveway borders, pool-area landscaping, rental properties and homes preparing for sale.

Storm and Drainage Awareness

Landscaping That Accounts for Heavy Rain and Coastal Storms

No landscape can eliminate severe-weather risk, but design choices can reduce avoidable problems. Oversized plants too close to structures, poorly contained mulch on slopes and beds that redirect water toward buildings can create recurring issues.

  • Use mature plant size when determining distance from buildings and walkways.
  • Consider rock or stronger containment where mulch repeatedly washes out.
  • Avoid blocking drainage paths or trapping water against foundations.
  • Plan for seasonal cleanup after wind, heavy rain and tropical systems.
  • Address obvious irrigation leaks or grading concerns before installing premium materials.
Drainage note: Large or engineered stormwater problems may require a specialized drainage contractor or design professional.
Drainage & Site Preparation

Standing Water, Erosion, Grading & Problem Yards

Poor grading, downspout discharge, compacted soil and repeated washout can undermine an otherwise attractive landscape. We document visible trouble spots before installing new plants, mulch, rock or sod.

Minor bed reshaping, surface preparation and soil amendments may be part of an approved landscaping scope. French drains, major regrading, retaining structures, land clearing, stump removal and engineered stormwater solutions require separate evaluation and appropriately qualified contractors; they are not automatically included.
