The Coastal Question

Mulch or Rock? Here's the Honest Answer

Mulch is the better gardener: it feeds our nutrient-poor sandy soil as it decomposes, insulates roots through July heat, and holds moisture between rains. Its cost is recurrence — coastal sun and storms break it down, so plan on topping off every year or two. Rock is the better investment: it never washes down the driveway in an August downpour, never scatters in tropical-storm wind, and never fades — but it feeds nothing, so it belongs around established shrubs and in high-wash zones, not young plantings.

Most Grand Strand yards we build use both: rock at borders, downspout zones, and slopes; mulch where things are still growing. We'll tell you which goes where at the estimate — including when the cheaper option is genuinely the right one.

Questions

Landscaping FAQ

How much does landscaping cost in Myrtle Beach?

Straight from our menu: bush trimming from $150, mulch from $350, refreshes from $500, rock from $750, sod from $3,500. Free on-site estimates turn those into exact written numbers before anything starts.

Mulch or rock for my beds?

Mulch feeds sandy soil and protects plantings but needs refreshing every year or two; rock is permanent and storm-proof but feeds nothing. Most coastal yards do best with rock in wash-out zones and borders, mulch around active plantings — we'll map it at the estimate.

What's included in the $500 landscape refresh?

Re-edging and weeding every bed, trimming and shaping shrubs, topping off mulch or rock, and full debris haul-away. It's the highest-impact single visit for listings, rentals, and yards that got away from someone.

When do you trim shrubs here?

Main shaping in late winter/early spring, maintenance cuts through the season, and spring bloomers like azaleas immediately after flowering. Species first, calendar second.

Can you maintain it after the install?
