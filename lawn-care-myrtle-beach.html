Why Myrtle Beach Is Different

Lawn Care in Myrtle Beach Demands More Than a Standard Mow

Myrtle Beach sits in a warm, humid coastal zone where grass growth accelerates from March through October — often requiring weekly mowing during peak summer months just to stay ahead of it. Salt air from the Atlantic deposits mineral residue on grass blades and soil, gradually stressing turf that isn't properly maintained. Sandy soil drains quickly, making it difficult to retain moisture and nutrients without consistent care.

Add in the heavy foot traffic of vacation rental properties, the rapid regrowth after summer storms, and the unique mix of warm-season grasses that dominate Horry County — Bermuda, St. Augustine, Centipede, and Zoysia — and you have a lawn environment that rewards consistent, knowledgeable maintenance and punishes neglect quickly.

Myrtle Beach Grass Guide

The 4 Grasses We Mow in Myrtle Beach

Each grass type thrives differently in Myrtle Beach's coastal climate. We adjust mowing height and frequency based on what's growing in your yard.

🌿

Bermuda Grass

Extremely heat-tolerant and fast-growing. Needs frequent mowing during summer — sometimes weekly. Handles foot traffic well, popular in vacation rentals and high-traffic yards.

Mow every 5–7 days in summer
🏖️

St. Augustine Grass

The most common coastal grass in Myrtle Beach. Tolerates salt spray better than most varieties — ideal for oceanfront and ocean-view properties. Needs consistent watering in sandy soil.

Best for coastal/salt-air properties
🌱

Centipede Grass

Thrives in Myrtle Beach's acidic, sandy coastal soils. Lower maintenance than Bermuda or St. Augustine — slower growing and naturally weed-resistant. Popular in residential yards.

Lower maintenance · Sandy soil friendly
☀️

Zoysia Grass

Dense, drought-tolerant and handles Myrtle Beach sun well. Slower-growing than Bermuda which means less frequent mowing — a good option for homeowners wanting a tidy yard without weekly cuts.

Drought-tolerant · Less frequent mowing
Coastal Lawn Challenges

What Makes Myrtle Beach Lawn Care Uniquely Demanding

Myrtle Beach lawns face a combination of stressors that inland properties never deal with. Here's what we specifically account for at every visit.

🌊

Salt Air & Spray

Atlantic salt air deposits residue on grass blades and soil that gradually stresses turf, dulls color, and accelerates thatch buildup — especially on oceanfront and ocean-view properties.

🏜️

Sandy Soil

Myrtle Beach's sandy coastal soil drains quickly and struggles to retain moisture and nutrients. Grass in sandy conditions grows in patches and requires consistent cutting to stay uniform.

💧

High Humidity & Rapid Growth

Summer humidity accelerates grass growth dramatically. Properties left for 2+ weeks can go from neat to overgrown — weekly service is the only reliable way to stay ahead of it.

⛈️

Storm & Hurricane Debris

Summer storms scatter leaves, palm fronds, and debris across lawns. Proper debris cleanup after mowing keeps surfaces clear and prevents clippings from smothering grass.

🏠

Vacation Rental Curb Appeal

In Myrtle Beach's competitive short-term rental market, curb appeal drives guest impressions. A neat lawn signals a well-maintained property and directly influences reviews.

🌴

Palm & Tropical Growth

Palm fronds, ornamental tropicals, and fast-growing coastal shrubs create trimming demands around beds and borders that standard inland lawn services don't typically handle.
