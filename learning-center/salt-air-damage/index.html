How Salt Air Damages Your Myrtle Beach Home

5 min read Myrtle Beach & Horry County, SC By Bakerss Property Maintenance

If you've ever left a metal tool outside near the Myrtle Beach coast and found it rusted in a week, you already understand the power of salt air. The same corrosive process that destroys tools attacks every exterior surface of your home — just more slowly and more subtly. Understanding what salt air does and how fast it works is the first step to protecting your investment.

What Is Salt Air and Why Is It Damaging?

Salt air refers to the microscopic salt particles carried inland from the ocean by wind. In Myrtle Beach, prevailing easterly winds mean these particles constantly deposit on every exposed surface — your siding, roof, windows, metal fixtures, driveway, deck, and landscaping. Salt is hygroscopic (it attracts and retains moisture) and corrosive to metals, and it provides a growth medium for the mold and mildew that thrive in Myrtle Beach's humid climate.

The impact intensity decreases with distance from the ocean, but properties up to 5–10 miles inland still experience measurable salt air effects. If you're within a mile of the beach, the impact is significant. If you're oceanfront, it's severe.

What Salt Air Damages — and How Fast

Exterior Paint

Paint on a Myrtle Beach home degrades 30–50% faster than inland paint due to salt air and UV exposure combined. Expect to repaint exterior surfaces every 5–8 years versus 10–15 years inland. Signs of salt air paint damage include chalking (powdery surface), peeling at edges and seams, and color fading especially on sun-exposed elevations.

Metal Components

Any metal that isn't marine-grade or properly sealed will corrode rapidly in coastal salt air. Affected items include: door hinges, exterior light fixtures, gutter hangers, window frames, garage door hardware, deck fasteners, and HVAC components. Stainless steel, aluminum, and hot-dip galvanized steel perform significantly better than standard steel or iron in coastal environments.

Siding and Exterior Surfaces

Salt deposits on siding create a film that retains moisture, accelerating mold and mildew growth. Vinyl siding chalks and fades faster in salt air. Wood siding absorbs salt moisture and rots more quickly than inland wood. Fiber cement siding (HardiePlank) performs better than wood but still needs regular washing to prevent salt buildup.

The biggest mistake Myrtle Beach homeowners make: Waiting until they can see damage before addressing it. Salt air damage is cumulative — every season without washing and maintenance accelerates the timeline to expensive repairs or replacement.

Windows

Salt etching on glass creates permanent hazing that can't be cleaned off once it sets. Window frames — especially aluminum — corrode and lose their seal, leading to air infiltration and moisture intrusion. Regular washing of window glass and frames prevents both salt etching and seal degradation.

Roofing

Asphalt shingles near the coast lose granules faster due to salt air and UV exposure combined. This accelerates the aging timeline of the roof by 2–5 years compared to inland installations. Algae and mildew growth — accelerated by salt-retained moisture — is also more aggressive in coastal roofing.

Prevention: What to Do and How Often

Pressure Washing / Soft Washing: Once to Twice Per Year

Regular exterior washing is the single most effective preventive maintenance measure for Myrtle Beach homes. Washing removes accumulated salt, mildew, and algae before they cause damage. Oceanfront properties should be washed twice yearly; homes 1–5 miles from the beach benefit from once yearly at minimum.

Painting: Every 5–8 Years with Coastal-Grade Products

Using premium exterior paints with mildewcide additives and high UV resistance is critical. Sherwin-Williams Emerald and Duration lines are designed for high-humidity, high-UV environments and last significantly longer than standard exterior paint in coastal conditions.

Metal Inspection and Treatment: Annually

Walk your property annually and inspect all metal components for early rust and corrosion. Treat early-stage rust immediately — sandblast or wire brush, apply rust converter, and repaint with metal primer and topcoat. Small rust spots become expensive repairs quickly in salt air environments.

Gutter Cleaning: Twice Per Year

Salt deposits in gutters combine with organic debris to accelerate corrosion of metal gutters. Regular cleaning extends gutter life and prevents the water damage that results from overflows.
