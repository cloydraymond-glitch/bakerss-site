Best Flooring for Myrtle Beach Vacation Rentals — 2026 Complete Guide

The flooring decision for a Myrtle Beach vacation rental is not primarily an aesthetic decision — it's an operational one. The wrong flooring in an active STR property means emergency replacement within 2–3 seasons, higher cleaning costs between stays, and guest complaints that suppress your review score. The right flooring means 15–20 years of performance, easier cleaning, better photographs, and higher occupancy.

This guide covers every major flooring type available for Myrtle Beach vacation rentals, with honest assessments of how each performs under beach conditions, heavy guest traffic, and the relentless humidity of coastal South Carolina.

The Short Answer — What to Use in Myrtle Beach Vacation Rentals

Main living areas, bedrooms, and hallways: 100% waterproof luxury vinyl plank (LVP) with a 12–20 mil wear layer and rigid SPC core. No exceptions.

All bathrooms and kitchens: Porcelain or ceramic tile. No exceptions.

What to avoid completely: Laminate flooring in any active STR application. Carpet in any high-traffic area. Standard hardwood or engineered hardwood in any Myrtle Beach vacation rental.

Why LVP Is the Only Correct Answer for Myrtle Beach STR Living Areas

Luxury vinyl plank with a waterproof SPC (stone-plastic composite) core is the material designed for exactly the conditions a Myrtle Beach vacation rental creates: constant foot traffic, wet feet from pool and beach access, beach sand acting as sandpaper against the wear surface, inconsistent temperature and humidity from guests leaving windows open or HVAC off, and cleaning with commercial-grade products between every stay.

Waterproof Core

LVP with an SPC core is genuinely 100% waterproof throughout its entire thickness — not just a surface coating. A wet towel left on the floor overnight, water tracked in from the pool, or a spilled drink doesn't damage the flooring at all. This eliminates the most common moisture damage scenario in vacation rentals.

Wear Layer Thickness

The wear layer is the clear protective surface on top of the printed layer. In residential applications, 6–8 mil wear layers perform adequately. In Myrtle Beach vacation rentals with 50+ guest stays annually, you need 12 mil minimum — 20 mil for maximum longevity. Beach sand is fine-grained silicon dioxide, essentially microscopic sandpaper. A thin wear layer doesn't last a full season under those conditions.

Looks Like Hardwood

Modern LVP is indistinguishable from hardwood in listing photographs — the digital printing technology creates realistic wood grain patterns across hundreds of design options. Guests don't know it's vinyl, your listing photos look premium, and your floors last 15–20 years instead of requiring replacement after 2–3 seasons.

What Happens When You Choose the Wrong Flooring

Laminate — The Most Common Mistake

Laminate flooring is not waterproof. Its HDF (high-density fiberboard) core swells when moisture penetrates the seams — and moisture always finds the seams in a Myrtle Beach vacation rental. Wet feet, mopping, and coastal humidity all drive moisture into laminate seams over time. Most Myrtle Beach STR operators who install laminate are replacing it within 2–4 seasons. The total cost (laminate installation + replacement with LVP + disposal of failed material) consistently exceeds the cost of installing LVP correctly from the start.

Carpet in High-Traffic Areas

Carpet in main living areas, hallways, and common areas of a beach vacation rental is a cleaning and health hazard. Beach sand embeds deeply in carpet fibers and cannot be fully removed by standard vacuuming — it accumulates through the life of the carpet, creating an abrasive layer at the base of the fibers that destroys the backing. Carpet also harbors allergens, bacteria, and biological material from hundreds of guest stays that standard between-stay cleaning cannot fully address. Carpet in bedrooms only is acceptable — everywhere else is a problem.

Standard Hardwood

Solid hardwood flooring expands and contracts significantly with humidity changes — and Myrtle Beach's coastal humidity swings are dramatic between occupied and unoccupied periods. Guests leave windows open in humid summer conditions. Owners turn the HVAC off entirely in winter. Both create humidity extremes that cause solid hardwood to cup, warp, gap, and eventually fail. Even engineered hardwood, which is more dimensionally stable than solid, is a significant maintenance burden and replacement risk in an active Myrtle Beach STR.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best flooring for a Myrtle Beach vacation rental?

100% waterproof luxury vinyl plank (LVP) with a rigid SPC core and 12–20 mil wear layer for all main living areas, bedrooms, and hallways. Porcelain or ceramic tile for all bathrooms and kitchens. Laminate should be avoided entirely in any active STR application in coastal SC.

Why is laminate a mistake in Myrtle Beach vacation rentals?

Laminate is not waterproof. Its HDF core swells when moisture penetrates the seams — and coastal humidity, wet feet from pool and beach access, and mopping all drive moisture into laminate seams. Most Myrtle Beach STR operators who install laminate replace it within 2–4 seasons at total cost higher than LVP would have been from the start.

How much does LVP installation cost in Myrtle Beach?

LVP installation starts at $3.50–$5.50 per square foot for standard 12-mil product. Premium 20-mil product runs $5.00–$7.50 per square foot. A typical 1,200 sq ft vacation rental in standard LVP runs $4,200–$6,600 all-inclusive. Free estimates for all Horry County properties.

Should I use carpet in my Myrtle Beach vacation rental?

Only in bedrooms, and only as a secondary option to LVP. Carpet in main living areas, hallways, and common areas accumulates beach sand, harbors bacteria and allergens from hundreds of guest stays, and cannot be adequately cleaned between turnovers. LVP throughout is a better long-term choice for most active STR properties.
