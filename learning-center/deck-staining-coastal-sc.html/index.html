Deck Staining & Sealing in Coastal SC — The Complete Guide

A wood deck in Myrtle Beach or Horry County faces more punishment per square foot than decks in almost any other region in the country. Salt air, intense UV, high humidity, and heavy rainfall combine to break down unprotected wood at an accelerated rate. The good news: regular staining and sealing dramatically extends deck life and keeps your outdoor space looking great year-round.

How Often Should You Stain Your Deck in Coastal SC?

  • Oceanfront and waterway properties: Every 1–2 years
  • Properties within 2 miles of the ocean: Every 2 years
  • Inland Horry County properties: Every 2–3 years

The simple test: sprinkle water on your deck. If it beads up, the sealer is still working. If it soaks in immediately, the deck is unprotected and needs attention.

What Happens When You Don't Stain

  • UV grays and bleaches the wood within one full summer — aesthetically unpleasant and structurally weakening
  • Moisture cycles cause wood to swell and contract, opening cracks and splitting boards
  • Algae and mildew establish in cracks and accelerate wood decay
  • Salt deposits draw additional moisture into the wood fiber, accelerating rot
  • Boards that could have lasted 20 years fail in 5–8 years
Bakerss Tip

Always pressure wash your deck thoroughly and allow it to dry completely before staining. Staining over dirt, mildew, or a wet surface seals the problem in rather than solving it — the stain will peel within a season.

Best Products for Coastal SC Decks

For most Horry County wood decks, we recommend semi-transparent penetrating stains rather than solid-color opaque stains. Penetrating stains soak into the wood fiber rather than sitting on top — they don't peel or crack when the wood expands and contracts with humidity, which is critical in our climate.

Top performers for coastal SC conditions include Armstrong Clark, TWP (Total Wood Preservative), and Defy Extreme — all formulated specifically for high-UV and high-humidity environments.

The Full Deck Prep and Staining Process

  • Pressure wash deck to remove dirt, mildew, and old stain residue
  • Allow minimum 48 hours dry time in coastal SC humidity
  • Sand any rough or raised grain areas
  • Apply wood brightener/cleaner to open grain and neutralize pH
  • Apply stain by brush, roller, or sprayer — back-brushing ensures penetration
  • Apply second coat where needed, especially on end grain
