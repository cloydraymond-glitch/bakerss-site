Deck Staining in Myrtle Beach, SC — Cost, Schedule & Product Guide 2026

Deck staining and sealing in Myrtle Beach is not optional maintenance — it's the difference between a deck that lasts 20 years and one that requires full board replacement within 7 to 10. Coastal South Carolina's combination of UV intensity, humidity, biological growth, and the occasional hurricane creates the most demanding deck maintenance environment on the East Coast. Getting the timing right, using the correct products, and preparing the surface properly determines whether your investment in a stain application actually protects the deck or just sits on the surface until it peels.

Why Myrtle Beach Decks Need More Frequent Maintenance

Wood decks in coastal SC face four simultaneous degradation forces that inland decks don't experience at the same intensity or frequency:

  • UV degradation: Coastal South Carolina has among the highest UV indices in the continental United States. UV breaks down the lignin in wood fiber, causing graying, checking (surface cracks), and erosion of the wood surface even on stained decks. South and west-facing deck surfaces show UV damage fastest.
  • Humidity and biological growth: Myrtle Beach's consistently high humidity keeps wood fiber at elevated moisture content, creating ideal conditions for mold, mildew, and fungal growth that accelerates wood rot. Decks that aren't regularly cleaned develop biological growth that penetrates the wood grain and causes staining that must be sanded rather than washed away.
  • Salt air: For oceanfront and near-ocean properties, salt air penetrates wood grain and creates the same hygroscopic moisture retention that affects other exterior surfaces — holding moisture in the wood continuously and accelerating biological growth.
  • Hurricane and storm exposure: Direct storm exposure deposits sand, organic debris, and standing water on deck surfaces that would rarely be seen on inland decks. Post-storm deck cleaning should be a standard part of storm recovery for any Myrtle Beach property with wood decking.

The Correct Deck Prep and Staining Process

Step 1 — Professional Pressure Washing

Before any stain application, the deck must be thoroughly cleaned to remove all dirt, biological growth, graying, and old stain residue. Professional deck washing uses appropriate pressure for the wood species (lower pressure for softer woods like cedar, higher for hardwoods) and a wood-safe cleaner that removes biological growth without damaging the wood fiber. This step cannot be skipped — stain applied over biological growth or gray weathered wood adheres poorly and fails within one season.

Step 2 — Brightening (if needed)

Weathered wood that has oxidized and grayed benefits from a wood brightener application after cleaning — an oxalic acid-based solution that neutralizes the pH of cleaned wood and opens the wood grain for better stain penetration. This step improves both the appearance and the durability of the stain application.

Step 3 — Drying

Wood must dry completely before stain application — typically 48–72 hours of dry weather after cleaning. Stain applied to wet wood traps moisture beneath the film and causes premature peeling. This is the most commonly rushed step in deck staining and the most common cause of premature failure.

Step 4 — Stain Application

For coastal SC conditions, penetrating oil-based or water-based penetrating stains outperform film-forming stains (solid stains and paint) because they soak into the wood rather than forming a surface film. Surface films peel as the wood beneath them expands and contracts with humidity cycles — a serious problem in Myrtle Beach's variable humidity environment. Penetrating stains allow the wood to breathe and wear rather than peel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a Myrtle Beach deck be stained?

Every 2–3 years for standard inland Horry County properties. Every 2 years for near-ocean properties. Annually or every 18 months for oceanfront direct-exposure decks. Annual cleaning regardless of staining schedule — biological growth removal protects the wood between stain applications.

How long should I wait after pressure washing to stain a deck?

48–72 hours minimum of dry weather. Wood must be completely dry before stain application — moisture trapped under stain film causes peeling within one season. This is the most commonly rushed step in deck staining and the most common cause of premature failure.

What type of stain is best for a Myrtle Beach deck?

Penetrating oil-based or water-based penetrating stains — not solid stains or paint-style products. Penetrating stains soak into the wood rather than forming a surface film. Surface films peel as coastal SC humidity cycles cause wood expansion and contraction. Penetrating products wear gradually rather than peeling.
