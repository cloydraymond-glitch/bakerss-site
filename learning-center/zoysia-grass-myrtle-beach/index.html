Zoysia Grass in Myrtle Beach, SC — Complete Care Guide 2026

Zoysia grass is the premium warm-season turf choice for Myrtle Beach and Horry County homeowners who prioritize appearance over everything else. Its fine texture, dense growth habit, and rich dark green color produce a lawn that looks more like a golf course putting surface than a suburban yard — which is exactly why it's the preferred grass in HOA communities like Grande Dunes, Barefoot Resort, and Carolina Forest neighborhoods where aesthetics are actively enforced. Understanding what Zoysia requires, where it excels, and where it falls short is key to deciding whether it's the right choice for your specific property.

What Makes Zoysia Different from Bermuda

✅ Zoysia Advantages

  • Finer, denser texture than Bermuda
  • Better shade tolerance (4–5 hrs sun)
  • Slower growth = less frequent mowing
  • Softer underfoot feel
  • Less aggressive lateral spread
  • Better cold tolerance than Bermuda
  • Lower water demand than St. Augustine

⚠️ Zoysia Limitations

  • Slower establishment (4–6 weeks vs 2–3)
  • Higher sod material cost
  • Slower traffic recovery than Bermuda
  • More thatch production
  • Later spring green-up than Bermuda
  • Not suitable for full shade
  • More expensive to maintain properly

Mowing Zoysia in Myrtle Beach

Mowing height for Zoysia depends on the specific variety — Empire Zoysia (the most common residential variety in Horry County) performs best at 1.5–2.5 inches. Zeon Zoysia, a finer-textured premium variety, is best at 0.75–1.5 inches and looks most like a golf course surface at proper height.

Because Zoysia grows more slowly than Bermuda, it doesn't require the same every-5-to-7-day mowing frequency during peak summer. Every 7–10 days during the peak growing season (May through September) maintains proper height for most Zoysia varieties. This slower growth rate is a practical advantage for busy homeowners — fewer service visits during peak season compared to Bermuda.

One important Zoysia-specific mowing note: Zoysia produces significantly more thatch than Bermuda. Annual dethatching is more important for Zoysia lawns than for Bermuda. Thatch above 0.5 inch in a Zoysia lawn reduces water infiltration, creates fungal pressure, and causes the shallow rooting that makes the lawn vulnerable to summer drought stress.

Zoysia is a lighter feeder than Bermuda — over-fertilizing Zoysia promotes thatch accumulation and creates disease pressure. Two applications per season is adequate for most Horry County Zoysia lawns; three is the maximum.

Where Zoysia Excels in Myrtle Beach

HOA communities with active aesthetic enforcement — Grande Dunes, Barefoot Resort, Wachesaw Plantation, and similar premium communities where lawn appearance is the primary metric. Zoysia's fine texture and dense carpet appearance is what HOA-enforced communities typically look like when maintained properly.

Properties with partial shade — Zoysia tolerates 4–5 hours of direct sun, making it appropriate for lots with significant tree coverage where Bermuda would thin out. Properties along Conway's Waccamaw River corridor or under Murrells Inlet's established oak canopy where Bermuda doesn't perform well are strong Zoysia candidates.

Lower-traffic residential properties — Zoysia recovers from wear more slowly than Bermuda. For a primary residence lawn that sees normal family activity, Zoysia is fine. For a vacation rental with heavy guest traffic throughout peak season, Bermuda's faster recovery rate is the better specification.
