🏖️ Property Owner's Guide to Myrtle Beach, SC — 2026

Myrtle Beach is the center of Horry County's economy, tourism, and real estate market — and the most maintenance-intensive property environment on the Grand Strand. Direct Atlantic frontage on its eastern edge, a 60+ mile coastal strip of vacation rental inventory, more than 100 golf courses across the metro area, and a year-round subtropical climate combine to make Myrtle Beach a city where professional property maintenance isn't optional — it's the difference between a property that holds its value and one that visibly deteriorates within seasons.

Myrtle Beach's Distinct Property Markets

Myrtle Beach is not a single uniform market — it's a collection of distinct communities, each with its own maintenance profile:

Grande Dunes is the city's premium address — Intracoastal Waterway frontage, two golf courses, a marina, and luxury homes with correspondingly premium maintenance expectations. Grande Dunes HOA enforcement is active and detailed. Properties here need professional weekly lawn care during growing season, consistent exterior upkeep, and the kind of attention to detail that matches the community's visual standard.

Carolina Forest is Myrtle Beach's largest master-planned community — 25,000+ residents across dozens of sub-neighborhoods, each with its own HOA layer. The scale of Carolina Forest creates consistent demand across every service category. Lawn care at HOA-standard frequency is the most common request; gutter cleaning under Carolina Forest's tree canopy is also elevated demand.

Market Common is the redeveloped former Air Force base — a walkable urban-style neighborhood with a distinct aesthetic and active HOA. Smaller lot sizes and dense landscaping create a different maintenance profile from standard suburban Myrtle Beach.

Oceanfront and near-ocean corridors — Broadway at the Beach area, the Ocean Boulevard tourist strip, and the residential neighborhoods immediately behind the ocean — face maximum salt air exposure and the highest exterior maintenance demands in the city.

Forestbrook and Socastee-adjacent Myrtle Beach neighborhoods share the heavy canopy conditions that characterize inland Horry County — elevated gutter demand, consistent lawn care needs, and less salt air exposure than oceanfront corridors.

What Makes Myrtle Beach Maintenance Different

Every coastal maintenance challenge is amplified in Myrtle Beach by the sheer concentration of property and the competitive vacation rental market. A clean, well-maintained exterior isn't just aesthetics — it's the first impression that 20 million annual visitors form before they decide whether to book, rent, or buy in this market. Properties that look maintained command better prices, better bookings, and better tenants. Properties that look neglected in a market this competitive lose ground fast.

Myrtle Beach's STR market — one of the largest and most competitive on the East Coast — creates specific maintenance pressure for vacation rental investors. Guests are comparing your property to thousands of competitors with a single tap. The property that wins bookings is the one that looks professionally maintained in photos and delivers on that impression at arrival. That standard requires consistent professional upkeep, not reactive repair.

How often should Myrtle Beach homes be pressure washed?

Annual is the minimum for most Myrtle Beach residential properties. Oceanfront and near-ocean properties benefit from twice-yearly washing. After any major storm, post-storm washing should be scheduled as soon as the property is safe to access.
