How Long Does Exterior Paint Last in Coastal South Carolina?

The honest answer is: less long than the paint can says. Exterior paint in coastal South Carolina faces a combination of stressors — salt air, intense UV radiation, high humidity, and heavy rainfall — that accelerate degradation significantly compared to inland or northern climates.

Here's what to realistically expect, and how to maximize the lifespan of any exterior paint job in Myrtle Beach and Horry County.

Realistic Lifespan by Surface Type

  • Vinyl siding: Paint typically lasts 5–7 years in coastal SC before fading, chalking, or peeling
  • Wood siding: 4–6 years with good prep; 2–3 years with poor prep or cheap paint
  • Fiber cement (HardiePlank): 8–12 years with quality paint and proper prep
  • Masonry and stucco: 5–10 years depending on crack maintenance and primer quality
  • Trim and shutters: 3–5 years — high UV exposure accelerates fading
The Big Variable

Distance from the ocean matters enormously. Properties within half a mile of the beach or waterway experience salt corrosion that can cut paint lifespan nearly in half compared to properties just a few miles inland. If you're in Grande Dunes, Cherry Grove, or Garden City Beach — plan for shorter intervals.

What Shortens Paint Life in Coastal SC

  • Salt air: Salt is abrasive and hygroscopic — it draws moisture into the paint film and accelerates breakdown at a molecular level
  • UV intensity: The Grand Strand receives more intense UV radiation than most US regions — it bleaches pigments and breaks down binders faster
  • Humidity cycling: Paint expands and contracts with daily humidity changes — over thousands of cycles, this creates micro-cracks that let moisture in
  • Algae and mildew: Coastal humidity allows biological growth that feeds on paint binders and causes premature failure
  • Poor prep: Painting over salt deposits, mildew, or chalked paint dramatically shortens the new coat's lifespan

How to Make Your Exterior Paint Last Longer

  • Use paint with a built-in mildewcide — essential in coastal SC
  • Always pressure wash before painting and allow full dry time
  • Apply two coats — the second coat doubles UV and moisture protection
  • Caulk every seam and joint before painting — water infiltration is the primary cause of paint failure
  • Choose 100% acrylic latex — it handles humidity cycling better than oil-based paints
  • Annual light pressure wash to remove salt and biological deposits extends paint life significantly

The Best Paints for Coastal SC

We use Sherwin-Williams Duration and Emerald lines for most exterior coastal work — both include built-in mildewcide and are formulated for high-humidity environments. Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior is another premium option with excellent coastal durability. In both cases, the paint itself is only as good as the prep work behind it.
