🌊 Property Owner's Guide to Surfside Beach, SC — 2026

Surfside Beach — known across the Grand Strand as 'The Family Beach' — is a small incorporated town between Myrtle Beach and Murrells Inlet with a strong permanent-resident base, the gated Oceanside Village community, and a mix of family vacation rentals and established coastal neighborhoods. Its residential character sets it apart from the commercial intensity of Myrtle Beach while sharing the same coastal maintenance challenges that define all South Grand Strand property ownership.

About Surfside Beach

Oceanside Village is Surfside Beach's most prominent community — gated, with an active HOA and one of the South Grand Strand's most established residential populations. The direct oceanfront neighborhoods along Ocean Boulevard face maximum salt air exposure and require the most aggressive exterior maintenance schedules. Deerfield and the inland neighborhoods behind Hwy 17 Business have a quieter, more permanent-resident character with year-round lawn care and maintenance demand.

Property Maintenance in Surfside Beach — What's Different Here

Surfside Beach's direct Atlantic frontage means full coastal maintenance conditions for all oceanfront and near-ocean properties. Salt air, UV intensity, and humidity combine to accelerate paint, exterior hardware, and surface degradation faster than inland properties. Annual pressure washing is a minimum for all Surfside Beach properties — twice yearly for oceanfront and near-ocean locations.

The 'Family Beach' identity brings a steady vacation rental market with family-sized properties that cycle through guests and need reliable turnover cleaning, exterior upkeep, and consistent lawn care to maintain the presentation that generates positive reviews. Oceanside Village's HOA standards create specific lawn care expectations that require professional, consistent service to meet.

How often should Surfside Beach homes be pressure washed?

Annual pressure washing is the minimum for standard Surfside Beach properties. Oceanfront and near-ocean properties benefit from twice-yearly washing due to the salt air and UV conditions that accelerate surface degradation.
