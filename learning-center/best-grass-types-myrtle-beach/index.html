Best Grass Types for Myrtle Beach, SC Lawns

Choosing the right grass for a Myrtle Beach lawn is one of the most important decisions a Horry County homeowner can make — and it's one where a lot of people get it wrong. Plant the wrong variety and you'll spend the whole summer fighting thinning turf, brown patches, and fungal problems. Plant the right one, and your lawn almost takes care of itself.

Here's what actually works in coastal South Carolina, based on years of lawn care experience across Myrtle Beach, Murrells Inlet, Surfside Beach, and Conway.

The Challenges of Coastal SC Lawns

Before picking a grass type, it helps to understand what you're working with. Horry County lawns face a combination of stressors that most other regions don't:

  • Sandy, well-draining soil that dries out fast and holds few nutrients
  • High humidity that promotes fungal diseases and lawn fungus
  • Salt air — especially within a few miles of the beach — that stresses grass blades
  • Intense UV and heat from May through September
  • Heavy rainfall during hurricane season followed by drought

Any grass you plant here needs to handle all of the above. The good news: several varieties do it exceptionally well.

Best Grass Types for Myrtle Beach Lawns

1. Zoysia Grass — The Best All-Around Choice

Zoysia is the most popular grass in Horry County for good reason. It handles the heat, tolerates drought once established, resists salt air reasonably well, and forms a dense, carpet-like turf that crowds out weeds. It goes dormant (turns tan) in winter but greens up beautifully in spring.

Bakerss Recommendation

Zoysia is our top pick for most Myrtle Beach homeowners — especially vacation rental owners who want a low-maintenance lawn that looks great from spring through fall.

  • Drought tolerant once established
  • Dense growth crowds out weeds naturally
  • Handles foot traffic well
  • Moderate salt tolerance
  • Slow to establish but very low maintenance long-term

2. Bermuda Grass — Best for Sun-Drenched Properties

Bermuda grass thrives in full sun and heat — two things coastal SC has in abundance. It's the most heat-tolerant option available and grows aggressively during summer, which means it recovers from damage quickly. It's a popular choice for commercial properties and high-traffic areas.

  • Excellent heat and drought tolerance
  • Recovers quickly from wear and damage
  • Fast-growing during peak season
  • Requires more frequent mowing in summer
  • Does not tolerate shade well

3. St. Augustine Grass — Best for Shaded Yards

If your property has significant tree coverage or shade, St. Augustine is your best option. It's the most shade-tolerant warm-season grass available and handles coastal humidity well. It does need more water than Zoysia or Bermuda and is susceptible to chinch bugs — a common pest in Myrtle Beach.

  • Best shade tolerance of any coastal SC grass
  • Handles humidity well
  • Attractive, wide-blade appearance
  • Higher water requirement
  • Watch for chinch bugs in summer heat

4. Centipede Grass — The Low-Maintenance Option

Centipede is sometimes called "the lazy man's grass" because it needs very little fertilizer and grows slowly — which also means less mowing. It's a reasonable choice for homeowners who want a low-input lawn, though it's more sensitive to salt and doesn't handle heavy traffic as well as Zoysia or Bermuda.

  • Very low fertilizer requirement
  • Slow-growing — fewer mowing trips needed
  • Good drought tolerance
  • Less suitable for oceanfront or salt-exposed properties
  • Sensitive to over-fertilizing (will burn)

What to Avoid

Fescue grasses (tall fescue, fine fescue) are designed for cooler climates. They consistently fail in Myrtle Beach summers — heat, humidity, and fungal pressure kill them out quickly. If you see a neighbor's lawn struggling through August, it's probably fescue. Stick with warm-season varieties.

The Bottom Line

For most Horry County properties, Zoysia is the right call. It looks great, handles coastal conditions well, and requires less ongoing maintenance than the alternatives. For full-sun commercial or high-traffic properties, Bermuda is the stronger performer. Shaded yards should go with St. Augustine.
