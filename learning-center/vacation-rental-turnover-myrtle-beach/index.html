Vacation Rental Turnover Guide — Complete Protocol for Myrtle Beach STR Properties

The vacation rental turnover is the most time-pressured operation in property management. You have a 4–6 hour window between guest checkout and guest check-in to reset the property completely — cleaning every surface, laundering all linens, inspecting for damage, restocking consumables, and staging everything back to listing photos. Do it well consistently and you get 5-star cleanliness reviews that drive bookings. Do it inconsistently and the review score erodes in ways that are slow to recover.

This guide covers the complete Myrtle Beach vacation rental turnover process from the moment a guest checks out to the moment the next guest walks in — including the beach-specific steps that separate Grand Strand turnover cleaning from inland STR protocols.

The Turnover Timeline — Myrtle Beach Peak Season

Standard Myrtle Beach summer checkout/check-in: 10 AM checkout, 4 PM check-in. That's a 6-hour window for a full property reset. During peak summer, same-day consecutive turnovers are the norm, not the exception. The team that executes this consistently — without rushing visible areas while neglecting corners — is the team that earns 5-star cleanliness reviews.

The Complete Turnover Process

Step 1 — Entry and Assessment (First 5 Minutes)

Walk the entire property immediately after entering — every room, bathroom, and outdoor area. Note any damage, missing items, or unusual conditions before touching anything. Photograph anything that may be an insurance or damage claim. This assessment happens before cleaning begins — documented damage is the basis for any guest charge back, and it must be documented before the cleaning disturbs the evidence.

Step 2 — Strip and Start Laundry

Strip all beds immediately — sheets, pillowcases, and any used towels — and start the first laundry load. Laundry is the longest-cycle task in the turnover. Starting it first means clean linens are ready before cleaning is complete. Most Myrtle Beach vacation rentals with multiple bedrooms need 3–4 wash/dry cycles — plan the turnover sequence around the laundry timeline.

Step 3 — Kitchen First

Clean the kitchen before any other room. Dishes in the sink, inside the dishwasher, inside the microwave, stove top, oven, refrigerator (remove guest food and wipe shelves), and countertops. The kitchen takes the most time and generates the most cross-contamination risk if left while other cleaning proceeds. A clean kitchen sets the standard for the rest of the property.

Step 4 — Bathrooms

Full bathroom cleaning: toilet inside and out, sink and counter, shower/tub surfaces and floor, mirrors, floor mopped, trash emptied. Check shower drain for hair — the most commonly cited bathroom cleanliness complaint in STR reviews. Replace toiletries to minimum levels and fold toilet paper end into a V for the hospitality presentation touch guests notice.

Step 5 — The Beach Sand Protocol

Before vacuuming or mopping any room: remove all cushions from sofas, chairs, and outdoor furniture and vacuum each cushion on all sides. Sand settles into cushion crevices and falls to the floor when guests sit — cleaning the floor without cleaning the cushions means sand is back on the floor within hours. After cushions, vacuum all floors thoroughly before any mopping. Sand on a wet floor creates gritty residue that guests feel through socks and bare feet. Check drawers, cabinet interiors, and beach gear storage areas for sand accumulation.

Step 6 — Bedrooms and Living Areas

Dust all surfaces — ceiling fans (the most commonly neglected area), shelving, window sills, light fixtures. Wipe all hard surfaces. Make beds with fresh linens from laundry. Stage bedroom to listing photos — throw pillows straight, nightstand items arranged. Check under beds for items left by previous guests.

Step 7 — Floors

Vacuum all carpeted areas. Vacuum hard floors before mopping (sand removal). Mop all hard floors. Pay attention to corners, baseboards, and areas beneath furniture where sand and debris accumulate.

Step 8 — Outdoor Areas

Sweep or blow porch, patio, and outdoor deck areas. Wipe outdoor furniture — pollen and salt air deposit continuously on all outdoor surfaces. Check outdoor shower for sand and debris. Pool deck if applicable.

Step 9 — Restock and Stage

Restock all consumables to agreed minimums: toilet paper, paper towels, dish soap, hand soap, trash bags, coffee. Replace welcome amenities if included in your listing. Stage property to listing photo positions — guests compare their arrival to the listing photos and any deviation registers as a negative.

Step 10 — Final Walk-Through

Walk every room with a critical eye before leaving. Check the same items guests check in the first 60 seconds: general visual impression, any visible dirt or debris, smells, temperature setting. Photograph one room per turnover as documentation of completion condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Myrtle Beach vacation rental turnover take?

A standard 2BR/2BA Myrtle Beach vacation rental takes 2.5–4 hours for a professional team. 3BR/2BA runs 3.5–5 hours. Larger 4BR+ properties run 4–7 hours depending on size and condition. The 10 AM–4 PM same-day window is tight for larger properties with multiple bathrooms.

What is the most important part of a Myrtle Beach STR turnover?

The beach sand protocol — removing sand from cushions, floors, drawers, and storage areas before the next guest arrives. Sand on floors and furniture is the most commonly mentioned cleanliness issue in Myrtle Beach STR reviews, and it comes entirely from inadequate sand removal rather than general dirtiness.

Can you sync turnover cleaning with my Airbnb calendar?

Yes — Bakerss receives access to your rental calendar via shared iCal link. Cleaning is dispatched automatically on every checkout day without any coordination from you. This is how out-of-state STR owners maintain consistent turnover cleaning without managing each booking individually.

What happens if a guest leaves the property in bad condition?

During the walk-through step, we document condition with photos before cleaning begins. Any damage, excessive mess surcharges, or missing items are reported immediately so the owner can file a claim or request before the next guest arrives. We do not clean around unreported damage — documentation first is the correct professional protocol.
